How Long Does Drywall Take? Real Timelines for Oakville Homes and Commercial Projects

📅 August 2025⏱ 5 min read✍ LR Contracting
Home›Blog›How Long Does Drywall Take

How long will the drywall take? It is one of the most important planning questions on any build or renovation — and the answer depends on the scope, crew size, finish level and conditions on site. Here are real timelines based on our work across Halton Region.

Vaulted ceiling drywall installation on an Oakville renovation — high ceilings and special geometry add time to the schedule but are part of our everyday scope.

The Phases That Make Up the Drywall Timeline

  • Board hanging — physically cutting and screwing drywall panels to framing
  • First coat taping — embedding tape and first compound coat on all joints
  • Drying time — 24 hours minimum per coat; more in cold or humid conditions
  • Second coat — fill coat, feathered wider than first
  • Third coat — finish coat, feathered to full width
  • Sanding — between each coat and after final coat
  • Level 5 skim (if specified) — full surface skim coat plus final sanding pass

Even the most experienced crew in Oakville cannot rush drying time. Joint compound that is not fully dry will shrink, crack, and cause failures that appear months later under paint. This is the most important thing to understand about drywall timelines — and why contractors who promise unrealistically fast turnarounds are a red flag.

The most common cause of drywall delays

Other trades. Drywall cannot start until rough electrical, plumbing, HVAC and insulation are complete and inspected. The drywall contractor showing up on time only matters if every trade before them finished on time. As a GC or homeowner, protecting your drywall start date means protecting every preceding trade's deadline.

What Adds Time to the Schedule

  • Cold weather: Below 13°C, joint compound will not cure properly. In winter builds, the space must be heated before and during taping.
  • High humidity: Common in Halton Region summers. Fans and dehumidifiers help but cannot eliminate extended drying time entirely.
  • Complex ceilings: Coffered ceilings, curved walls and multi-level tray ceilings add 30–50% to taping time compared to flat standard ceilings.
  • Level 5 finish: Add 2–4 working days to any timeline for Level 5 versus Level 4. The skim coat and final sanding are significant additional steps.
