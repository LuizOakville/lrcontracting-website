Finishing a basement in Oakville is one of the highest ROI home improvement projects available to Ontario homeowners — adding usable square footage at a fraction of the cost of an addition. Drywall is the biggest visible component, and doing it right below grade requires specific knowledge that differs from above-grade work.

Moisture-Resistant Drywall is Non-Negotiable Below Grade

Standard drywall has a paper facing that absorbs moisture and develops mould in a basement environment, even when you cannot see it. For all basement walls — and ceilings over wet areas — you must use moisture-resistant drywall (green board, blue board, or DensArmor depending on manufacturer). In bathrooms and laundry rooms, cement board or a waterproofing system is required.

The Right Insulation Sequence for Basement Walls

In Ontario's climate, basement walls require careful insulation sequencing to avoid moisture problems inside the wall assembly:

  1. Foundation wall — concrete or block
  2. Spray foam applied directly to the concrete — the primary thermal and vapour control layer
  3. Stud wall framed away from concrete — typically 2x4 at 16 inches OC
  4. Optional batt insulation in stud cavities — only when spray foam is the primary vapour control layer
  5. Moisture-resistant drywall on the stud wall
The most common basement insulation mistake

Stapling poly vapour barrier directly against the concrete foundation wall, then framing in front of it. This traps moisture between the poly and the concrete, creating the exact conditions mould needs. Spray foam applied directly to the concrete is the correct approach.

Ceiling Options for Basement Finishing

Drywall Ceiling

  • Higher-end residential look
  • Maximizes ceiling height
  • Painted to match walls
  • More expensive to install
  • Requires drywall patch for any plumbing access

T-Bar Drop Ceiling

  • Easy access to pipes and wiring above
  • Fast to install
  • Commercial look, not residential
  • Lower cost
  • Good for utility and mechanical zones
